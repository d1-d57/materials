Functors and natural transformations

3.1

Functors

A functor is a morphism between categories, the standard example is the List
functor which is an endofunctor on the category of sets, i.e. it is a functor from
Set to Set, I write List : Set ! Set overloading !6 . The list functor has an
e↵ect on objects, i.e. sets, given A : Set we obtain List A : Set which is the set
of lists over A which we write as [a0 , a1 , . . . , an 1 ], this includes the empty list
[]. The List functor comes with a function which “maps” a function f : A ! B
to List f : List A ! List B, which is defined as
List f [a0 , a1 , . . . , an 1 ] = [f a0 , f a1 , . . . , f an 1 ]
Note that we overload List to mean at the same time a map on objects and a map
on morphisms. List also preserves the categorical structure, it maps identity to
identity, that is List idA = idList A and List (f g) = (List f ) (List g). This can
be easily seen by showing that both sides are pointwise equal when applied to
a generic list
List idA [a0 , a1 , . . . , an 1 ] = [id a0 , id a1 , . . . , id an 1 ]
= [a0 , a1 , . . . , an 1 ]

Inspired by List we define the notion of a functor in general: given categories
C and D a functor F : C ! D is given by a map on objects F : |C| ! |D|
and a map on morphisms F : C(A, B) ! D(F A, F B) that preserves identities
F idA = idF A and composition F (f g) = (F f ) (F g).

Categories form another structure, a higher category. Functors between two

polymorphic functions.

The monoid constructed with lists is special, it is the free monoid over a

subject to any other laws. There are other functors with the type Set ! Mon
for example the finite multisets or the finite sets over a given set. These do
satisfy additional laws, the counterpart to the append operation is commutative
in the case of multisets l ++m = m ++l and idempotent in the case of finite sets
l ++l = l, hence they are not free over monoids.
